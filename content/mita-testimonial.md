### 01 媽媽，房間可以整理一下嗎？
聊生活環境，米塔的重點很明確：東西少一點、走路方便一點。媽媽笑說，沒想到除了自己的媽媽，還會被她催整理。

**Mum, could you tidy the room?**
Mita had a clear request: fewer things and more room to walk. Mum laughed—now her dog was reminding her to tidy up, too.

### 02 這題先不要驗證啦！
媽媽不好意思拍房間，想請米塔聊點別的。結果連道別前都繞回同一題，最後媽媽笑著承認：「房間是真的很亂！」

**Can we skip this one?**
Mum was too embarrassed to send a room photo and tried to change the subject. Mita kept coming back to it. In the end, Mum laughed: “The room really is messy!”

### 03 嘴上嫌弟弟，心裡卻懂媽媽。
米塔一直嫌弟弟太大隻，卻沒有怪媽媽偏心。我轉述她知道弟弟需要更多照顧，媽媽也認出了這份熟悉的體貼。

**She complains, but she understands.**
Mita kept saying her little brother was too big. But when Mum worried about giving him more attention, I shared that Mita understood he needed more care.

## 完整故事｜中文

### 「除了整理房間，還有變小，謝謝。」

米塔的故事・日常溝通

以下依當次對話整理；米塔的話是我在溝通中的轉述，媽媽的確認與更正也一併保留。

米塔的媽媽原本想聊生活環境、喜歡的食物、朋友，以及有沒有什麼想對媽媽說的話。沒想到這場聊天有一個怎麼繞都繞不開的主題：媽媽，房間真的可以整理一下。

#### 房間是家，但走路要左閃右閃。

聊到家裡，我先問是不是出租屋，媽媽說不是。再慢慢釐清，米塔讓我想到的生活空間其實是房間。媽媽說房間確實小，大櫃子很多；遇到害怕的事，米塔也會躲回房間。

我問起收納櫃和紙箱，媽媽回答兩個都有，還形容走道只剩小小一條。我轉述那種走路得左閃右閃的感覺，接著冒出一句：「媽媽東西可以少一點」。

媽媽笑問：「現在是 微出賣媽媽嗎」。她還說，沒想到這句話除了能從自己的媽媽口中聽到，還會從米塔這裡聽到。原本的生活訪談，就這樣變成小狗的整理房間提醒。

#### 媽媽想換題，米塔卻沒有忘記。

後來再問米塔有什麼想說的，我轉述的仍然是：「房間變大一點」。我又描述床邊有東西和紙箱的畫面，媽媽終於笑著抗議：「能不能稍微除了這件事以外講別的！！！」

說到拍照，她回：「礙於太亂我真的是很不好意思拍給你看🤣」。這裡也有一個要保留的更正：我原先以為是米塔的床，媽媽說「床是我的床」，並確認四面都是櫃子。所以這段不是每個位置都被照片核對過，而是媽媽在對話中確認了擁擠、雜物多的狀況，也修正了床的身分。

當媽媽再問還有什麼願望，我轉述米塔想「把弟弟變小」。媽媽立刻補上：「除了整理房間」「還有變小」「謝謝」。那個努力把兩個選項排除的口吻，實在太可愛。

#### 道別可以，整理房間還是要提醒。

就連說完愛、準備道別，我又提到房間的畫面。媽媽笑回：「你不要亂傳送哈哈哈哈哈」。

結束後，她先說很開心，再開玩笑補一句「除了那個房間的部分」。但媽媽並沒有一直不給確認：後來她說「全部都驗證到！」，也明確寫下「房間是真的很亂！」。這段最好笑的，是媽媽想暫時跳過這題，米塔卻從頭到尾都很堅持。

#### 那顆曾經每天一起玩的網球。

房間之外，也有讓媽媽想起過去的小片段。我描述一顆黃綠色、像網球的東西，媽媽起初說球很久沒玩、已經不見了，接著想起米塔以前「無敵愛」網球。還沒有弟弟時，她們曾天天到公園玩你丟我撿。

媽媽也補充，現在因為米塔的關節狀況，不太敢讓她像以前那樣跑玩。後來她找到以前的影片，分享那段米塔愛玩球的日常。這個片段留下的，是她們一起生活過的回憶。

#### 嫌弟弟太大隻，也懂他需要照顧。

米塔的另一個重點是弟弟閃電太大隻。從房間擁擠聊到散步，我轉述她嫌弟弟容易大驚小怪；媽媽也確認了弟弟容易害怕、需要比較多照顧。

但當媽媽問米塔會不會覺得自己偏心，我轉述的答案是否定的：她知道弟弟需要更多關懷。媽媽說，之前也曾聽到米塔表達相同意思，接著回：「大愛你寶貝」。

這場聊天裡的米塔很有主見，催整理、嫌弟弟，又有自己的體貼。至於最後還有沒有別的重點？有的——記得房間。

## Full story｜English

### “Anything except tidying the room—or making him smaller. Thanks.”

Mita’s story · Everyday pet communication

This story is based on our conversation. Mita’s words are what I shared during the session; Mum’s responses and corrections are included too.

Mum wanted to ask about Mita’s home, favorite food, friends, and anything she wanted to say. We ended up returning to one topic again and again: Mum, could you please tidy the room?

#### Home, with a few obstacles.

I first asked if they lived in a rental. Mum said no. As we talked, the space I was describing became clearer: the bedroom. Mum said it was small and had lots of large cupboards. Mita also went there when she felt scared.

When I asked about storage cupboards and cardboard boxes, Mum confirmed they had both. She said the walkway was very narrow. I described having to weave around things, then shared: “Mum could have fewer things.”

Mum laughed and asked if Mita was giving away her secrets. She had never expected to hear the same tidying reminder from both her own mum and her dog.

#### Mum tries to change the subject.

Later, I asked if Mita had anything else to say. Back came the same idea: more room. When I described things and boxes around a bed, Mum laughed: “Can we please talk about something else?”

She said the room was so messy that she was too embarrassed to send a photo. There was a correction, too: I had thought it was Mita’s bed, but Mum said it was hers. She confirmed that cupboards surrounded it. So we did not verify every position in a photo; Mum confirmed the clutter and corrected which bed it was.

When Mum asked about any other wishes, I shared: “Make my little brother smaller.” Mum quickly added: anything except tidying the room or making him smaller, thanks. Her effort to take both options off the table was wonderfully funny.

#### One last reminder before goodbye.

Even after we had shared loving messages and were getting ready to finish, the room came up again. Mum laughed: “Stop sending that over!”

Afterward, she said she had really enjoyed the chat, then jokingly added, “Except for the room part.” But she did confirm it later, writing: “The room really is messy!” The joke was not that she kept denying it. She wanted to skip the topic, while Mita kept bringing it back.

#### A tennis ball and an old memory.

There was a softer moment, too. I described a small yellow-green ball that looked like a tennis ball. Mum said they had not played with it for a long time because it had gone missing. Then she remembered just how much Mita used to love it. Before her little brother arrived, they had played fetch at the park every day.

Mum explained that she now limited that kind of running because of Mita’s joints. Later, she found an old video and shared it—a little glimpse of their days playing together.

#### She complains about him, but she understands.

Mita also had plenty to say about her little brother, Shandian, being too big. I shared her complaints about him getting startled on walks. Mum confirmed that he was easily scared and needed more care.

When Mum asked if Mita thought she favored him, though, I shared that Mita understood he needed the extra attention. Mum said she had heard the same sentiment before, then told Mita how much she loved her.

Mita came across as a girl with her own opinions—and a caring side beneath the complaints. As for her final reminder? Yes, Mum. The room.
